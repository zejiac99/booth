# Thought Paper 1

**Word count: 539**

## a. Connection to Lecture

When I joined my previous employer, I was the first Asian employee on a team that had always been homogeneous. My colleagues were kind and supportive, yet I always felt a wall between us, and it was hard to fit in. The Class 1 material resonated with me because it explained what I ended up doing. The fundamental attribution error leads us to blame character when the situation is doing much of the work. My colleagues' character was never the problem. Our situation offered no shared, low-stakes setting where a newcomer and an established team could connect, so conversations stayed formal and cautious.

Instead of asking colleagues to change their attitudes, I changed one factor in the environment: I brought traditional Chinese dishes to work and invited people to try them. Food gave everyone an easy reason to gather and ask questions without a lecture about respect. Gradually, colleagues grew curious about Chinese culture, and the meals became shared memories. That common context built trust, and it reached our work: communication became smoother, conversations more organic, and collaboration easier.

As at NUMMI, changing the setting was cheaper and more effective than changing the people.

## b. Response to Readings

The Heaths identify six principles of sticky ideas (SUCCESs):

- **Simple:** find the core and state it compactly, like a proverb.
- **Unexpected:** break expectations, then fill the gap with insight.
- **Concrete:** use sensory detail and human actions.
- **Credible:** carry its own credentials, through vivid details or testable claims.
- **Emotional:** make people feel for a person, not a statistic.
- **Stories:** a mental flight simulator for action.

The villain is the Curse of Knowledge: once we know something, we can't imagine not knowing it.

Questions:

1. Some work is inherently tedious, like fixing PowerPoint formatting. The Heaths say ideas are made interesting, not born that way. But making formatting sticky seems to require reframing it as more meaningful than it feels ("clients judge our numbers by how clean the pages look"). Is that finding the core, or a story we tell ourselves?
2. The Kidney Heist and the Halloween candy scare show the principles work as well for false, fearful ideas. In a polarized media environment, does optimizing for stickiness reward extremes?

## c. Connection to Readings

One example that comes to mind is Forrest Gump's line: "My mama always said life was like a box of chocolates. You never know what you're gonna get." It is simple: one sentence carries a core truth about uncertainty, like a proverb. It is unexpected: life, a heavy philosophical subject, becomes something ordinary and childlike. It is concrete: you can picture your hand hovering over the box. It is credible because anyone can test it against their own life, tasting each "chocolate" as it comes.

The line covers the last two principles as well. It is emotional because it comes from his mother, so it feels warm and comforting, and it sits inside a story: Forrest says it on a bench while offering a stranger a chocolate. That is probably why it has stuck with people for so long. It is easy to remember, easy to picture, and easy to apply to your own life.
