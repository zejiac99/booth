## The MaxHealth boundary

You flagged the real risk correctly. Getting this wrong in a JPM interview would be far worse than leaving the detail out entirely, so here's the line drawn explicitly.

**What you can say, truthfully:**

- You helped build the five-year enterprise strategy
- That included framing capital deployment priorities for the period
- Humana subsequently made an acquisition consistent with those priorities
- You had no role in the transaction itself

**What you must not imply:**

- That you shaped the *deal* thesis (you shaped the *capital* thesis — different object)
- That you were involved in target selection, diligence, valuation, or negotiation
- Any knowledge of deal mechanics, process, or non-public terms

The phrase from my earlier draft — "I helped shape the thesis" — was the exact failure mode you spotted. In context it reads as the MaxHealth thesis. Cut it.

**Three phrasings, from most to least specific.** Pick based on the room.

*Most specific — names the deal, distances clearly:*
> "This February, Humana acquired MaxHealth. I wasn't part of that deal — it ran through corp dev and an outside bank — but it was the kind of move the capital priorities we'd set pointed toward."

*Middle — the deal is present but unnamed:*
> "The company made an acquisition off the back of those priorities earlier this year. That work went to corp dev and an outside bank; my involvement ended at the strategy."

*Safest — no transaction referenced at all:*
> "My work ended at the strategy layer. When those priorities turned into actual transactions, that happened somewhere I wasn't."

The third version loses very little. The "why banking" logic — *I set direction, someone else did the interesting part* — works entirely without a named deal.

**Prepare the follow-up now.** If you name it, someone will ask about it. Your answer should redirect cleanly to what you legitimately know:

> "I should be upfront that I wasn't on that deal, so I can't speak to how it was run or priced. What I can speak to is why it fits — [strategic rationale from your own five-year work]."

That answer is *strong*, not weak. Volunteering the boundary reads as integrity, and the strategic rationale is genuinely your expertise. The failure mode is bluffing and getting caught two questions deep.

**On JPM specifically.** If they ran it, someone in your interview loop may have been on the deal team. That's an argument for naming it *and* disclaiming it plainly rather than gesturing vaguely — vagueness is what looks evasive. It can also become a natural "why JPM" thread, framed carefully: you were on the strategy side of a house they advise, and you want to be on their side of that handoff. Say it that way, not in any way that implies you worked together.

**One caution on the dollar figure.** I dropped "$1B" from the drafts. Only use a number that came from a press release, filing, or news coverage — never one you know from being inside the company. If it's public, put it back; the specificity helps. If you're not certain it's public, leave it out. This is a small detail with asymmetric downside.

## Line edits I made to your draft

**"That initially led me to consulting. After college, I joined Humana's..."** — as written, this reads as though consulting *and* Humana are the same thing. Restored the choice: recruited for consulting, chose Humana instead.

**The nephrology clause** — "a partnership with one of the largest nephrology providers to improve operating efficiency and drive near-term earnings" attached the efficiency outcome to the partnership. Restored the AI use cases as the thing carrying that outcome, so nothing is misattributed.

**"Then I was asked to help building"** → "help build." Also restored the pure-play-insurer-to-consumer-healthcare-company detail, which you'd cut — without it, "five-year strategy" is abstract, and that one clause is what makes it concrete.

**"But there was a sense of unfulfillment lingered"** → replaced. The grammar aside, "unfulfillment" is doing something the concrete version does better: describing the actual handoff.

## Still to fill in

**Quantified impact.** The club's format asks for role *and impact*. Your Humana beats are still activities. One number in the enterprise strategy section would change how the middle of the pitch lands. Two is plenty. Public or non-sensitive figures only.

**Your differentiator, said out loud.** Four years inside a payer is unusual in an associate pool and directly relevant to healthcare coverage — you know how these decisions get made client-side. The pitch implies it. Consider making it explicit in the close, or holding it for "why should we hire you."

**Whether to name all three business-unit projects.** Three in a row can flatten into a list. Naming two and keeping the third in reserve gives you something fresh when they ask you to go deeper.
