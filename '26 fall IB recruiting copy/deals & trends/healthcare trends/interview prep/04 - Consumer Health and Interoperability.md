# 4. Consumer health platforms buy context and guidance — Oura / Function

Prepared October 6, 2026. This is a differentiated fourth path, with a strong strategic story and limited public deal economics. The chain is **measurement becomes an ongoing consumer relationship → buy data integration or guidance → follow Oura or Function**.

## The specific thesis

Consumer health platforms are expanding beyond isolated measurements into services that combine longitudinal data, medical context, and guidance. The acquisitions of interest add capabilities that make a recurring relationship more useful: medical-record integration, imaging, or information about consumers' supplement routines.

This is more specific than “healthcare is becoming consumer-facing.” The commercial hypothesis is that a useful next step increases engagement and renewal, supporting additional product sales. Improved health outcomes and lower medical costs remain separate propositions to prove.

## Mechanism and why now

Wearables create frequent measurements; labs and clinical records add information at less frequent intervals. Those datasets can be difficult to interpret together. A platform may buy the technology, talent, and integrations to connect them rather than develop every capability internally.

Rock Health's June 2026 analysis of its 2025 survey of 8,000 Census-matched US adults found 46% owned a wearable, versus 13% in 2015. It also found 59% of wearable owners had discussed their data with a provider. Adoption growth was plateauing, so the stronger “why now” is deeper usefulness and integration, not an assumption that first-time adoption will accelerate indefinitely. Survey results establish reported behavior, not clinical benefit. [Original survey analysis](https://rockhealth.com/insights/whats-your-score-insights-on-wearables-and-connected-devices-from-rock-healths-2025-consumer-adoption-survey/).

The local Morgan Stanley materials discuss patient-directed access, testing, and health spending. HTN provides a counterweight by questioning self-pay demand and the execution of consumer platforms. [Morgan Stanley consumer-health research](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/articles/healthcare-consumerism-drives-26-b-opportunity-2026).

> I'm following consumer health platforms expanding from measurement into connected guidance. ## Where interoperability connects to M&A

App-to-app sharing is the first step. A buyer is purchasing something more valuable if integration enables an understandable decision or useful workflow.

For consumer platforms, the questions are whether the connected information changes product use, renewal, or guidance. For VBC, the question is whether the information supports an intervention that improves care and cost performance. For RCM, it is whether clinical context prevents a documentation or payment problem.

Acquisition can bring integrations, data expertise, or distribution faster than internal development. Partnership can be preferable where maintaining a connection is enough and ownership adds little value. Do not assume every connected platform needs to acquire the others.

## Hard follow-ups

**Strategic or PE?** These are strategic capability acquisitions by consumer platforms; venture investors backed some targets. Public information does not establish a broad PE-led consumer-health rollup.

**Why might this fail?** More data can produce confusion, incidental findings, or limited actionable guidance. Consumers may not renew if they do not see ongoing utility, particularly when paying out of pocket.

**What would you diligence?** Cohort renewal, customer acquisition cost and payback, gross margin, product attach rates, actual data availability, integration reliability, consent/privacy controls, and evidence for claimed benefits. A compelling story is not enough to establish unit economics.

**Why not simply partner?** If data can be shared easily and the capability is not differentiating, partnership may be cheaper. Acquisition becomes more attractive when ownership of talent, technology, or a distinctive workflow creates value.

**Your opinion?** Favor targeted capabilities that turn existing data into a useful recurring product. Be skeptical of paying for breadth that fails to improve retention or outcomes.
